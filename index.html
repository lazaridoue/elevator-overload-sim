    How the overload logic works

    A load cell under the car floor reports total weight many times a second. The controller compares it to the rated load on the nameplate and switches between three modes.

    LoadModeDoorsCar
    under 80%NormalOpen at every callMoves normally
    80–100%Full-load bypassStay shut for hall calls; open only where riders get offPasses floors where people wait
    over 100%OverloadHeld openWon't leave. Buzzer and OVERLOAD light until someone steps off

    Your idea of keeping the doors shut when the car is full is the bypass mode. Real elevators can't weigh someone before they step in, so when the car tips over the limit the doors do the opposite and stay open, so the last person can get back out.

    The core rule, in Python

    RATED_KG    = 630    # nameplate: 8 persons x 75 kg
BYPASS_AT   = 0.80   # full-load bypass threshold
OVERLOAD_AT = 1.00

def on_load_reading(kg):
    ratio = kg / RATED_KG
    if ratio > OVERLOAD_AT:
        hold_doors_open()
        sound_buzzer()
        show_sign("OVERLOAD")
        block_departure()
    elif ratio >= BYPASS_AT:
        skip_hall_calls()     # don't stop for new riders
        allow_departure()
    else:
        answer_hall_calls()
        allow_departure()

    Build a desk-sized model

    • Bar load cell (5–20 kg) + HX711 amplifier as the scale under the car floor
    • Arduino Uno or ESP32 running the rule above
    • SG90 servo to slide the door, 28BYJ-48 stepper + ULN2003 driver to raise the car
    • Piezo buzzer, a red LED for OVERLOAD, push buttons for floor calls
    • Calibrate the scale with a known weight, then set a small rated load (say 500 g)
